In the fluorescent glow of a midnight diner,  
<br>
where the coffee churns like unspoken dreams,  
<br>
I sit in a booth stained with the laughter of strangers,  
<br>
glancing through the smudged glass  
<br>
at lives pieced together like broken mosaics.  
<br>

<br>
A father scribbles calculations on napkins,  
<br>
his tie askew like his faith in the flock,  
<br>
while his daughter tugs at her shirt,  
<br>
her eyes reflecting the flicker of a jukebox,  
<br>
filling silence with the ghosts of summer nights—  
<br>
the ones she wishes for in the cold embrace of winter.
<br>

<br>
Two lovers lean close like breaking waves,  
<br>
their words soft yet sharp as the clinking of cutlery,  
<br>
sharing secrets under the pressure of the ordinary,  
<br>
each glance a constellation,  
<br>
each sigh a gravity that pulls them closer  
<br>
to the magnetic fields of “What if?”  
<br>
and the horizon of “Why not?”
<br>

<br>
And the waitress,  
<br>
arms laden with trays heavy as history,  
<br>
sips her own cold, forgotten brew,  
<br>
her dreams unraveling like strands of spaghetti  
<br>
on a plate too hot for bare hands.  
<br>

<br>
Here, our narratives intertwine,  
<br>
the strangers and the regularly scheduled sirens,  
<br>
the harsh truth is the echo of laughter,  
<br>
fading as the seconds linger,  
<br>
like aging photographs trapped in a scrapbook  
<br>
of yesterdays irretrievable,  
<br>
yet palpably vivid, held in the mind's eye,  
<br>
shimmering like an old building,  
<br>
telling stories of the souls who dared to pass.  
<br>

<br>
So I gather my thoughts, shape them,  
<br>
not into rhymes, but into a prayer—  
<br>
for those lost in the birth of routine,  
<br>
and for those reaching out to grasp the sparks,  
<br>
the light that glimmers in the uncharted,  
<br>
a reminder that every moment is a seed  
<br>
waiting for the bloom of tomorrow.
